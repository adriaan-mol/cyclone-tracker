PART 2 : TROPICAL WEATHER DISCUSSION:

The short and temporary monsoon trough branch associated with the tropical low off the south-east of the Seychelles is still present this Thursday but is gradually moving away from the near-equatorial area and fed by weaker trans-equatorial flow near the equator. Convective activity is moderate to strong in the vicinity of the tropical low centered east-southeast of Agalega, which is now being monitored by RSMC La Reunion starting this Thursday 5 November at 12 UTC (Zone of Disturbed Weather 02-20262027). Convection is also weak to moderate elsewhere in the areas of trade winds deceleration.

Over the western part of the basin, enhanced vorticity and moisture content from the Rossby wave connected with the tropical low which is currently monitored will gradually shift westward then move away. Further east, equatorial easterly wind anomalies over the eastern part of the basin (influence from El Nino and weak positive IOD) are expected to recede in the coming days as an equatorial westerly wind burst is triggered by various equatorial waves.This enhancement of equatorial westerlies should indeed be favoured by the MJO regenerating over the basin, then by a Kelvin wave moving across between November 10th and 13th. An Equatorial Rossby wave should also move in from the east of the basin from November 11 or 12th, contributing to this westerly surge.This should enable a Near-Equatorial Trough (NET) pattern to settle over a large part of the basin from Sunday or Monday onwards, initially weak then getting better defined by the middle of next week.

10-day outlook:
By mid-November, the NET pattern should strengthen further more. A new tropical low pressure area could form somewhere between the centre or the east of the basin. Ensemble models hint a possibility of tropical storm formation, which will need to be confirmed.
