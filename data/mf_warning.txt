A BROAD LOW-PRESSURE AREA FORMED EARLIER THIS WEEK NORTHEAST OF
AGALEGA.
CONVECTION HAS GRADUALLY BECOME MORE ORGANIZED AND PERSISTENT SINCE
WEDNESDAY NIGHT JUSTIFYING AN INITIAL DVORAK CLASSIFICATION OF 1.0 ON
THURSDAY AROUND 3 UTC. OVER THE LAST 6 HOURS, CONVECTION HAS SLIGHTLY
WEAKENED IN CONNECTION WITH THE DIURNAL CYCLE BUT HAS PERSISTED NEAR
THE CENTER OF THE LOW-LEVEL CIRCULATION WITHOUT SHOWING ANY CLEAR
IMPROVEMENT IN ORGANIZATION. THE OUTFLOW OF CIRRUS CLOUDS TO THE
NORTH OF THE CIRCULATION INDICATES GOOD EQUATORIAL UPPER DIVERGENCE.
THE 0514Z ASCAT-B SWATH SHOWS A LOW LEVEL CIRCULATION THAT REMAINS
ELONGATED WITH A SLIGHTLY MORE PRONOUNCED CORE OF VORTICITY TO THE
SOUTHWEST OF THE CIRCULATION AND ESTIMATED MAXIMUM WINDS OF 25 KT.
THE GCOM-W MICROWAVE SWATH AT 0932Z SHOWS CONVECTION THAT IS STILL
POORLY ORGANIZED, CONSISTENT WITH THE STILL-ELONGATED STRUCTURE
OBSERVED BY ASCAT. THE FINAL T IS THEREFORE ESTIMATED AT 1.0 WITH AN
ESTIMATED INTENSITY OF ABOUT 25 KT.

IN TERMS OF TRACK, THE SYSTEM IS EXPECTED TO KEEP MOVING
SOUTHWESTWARD OVER THE NEXT 36 HOURS, ALONG THE EDGE OF A
LOW-TO-MID-TROPOSPHERIC RIDGE TO THE SOUTHEAST. THE SYSTEM IS THEN
EXPECTED TO TURN WESTWARD UNDER THE INFLUENCE OF A NEW SUBTROPICAL
RIDGE SOUTH OF MADAGASCAR. THE 00Z RUNS OF THE DETERMINISTIC AND
ENSEMBLE MODELS WERE IN GOOD AGREEMENT ON THE TRACK, ALTHOUGH THE IFS
AND EPS MODELS INDICATED A SLIGHTLY MORE SOUTHERLY TRACK. THE MOST
RECENT RUNS SHOW A SHIFT TOWARD THE NORTHWEST IN THE LONGER-TERM
TRACK, PROBABLY DUE TO PERSISTING UNCERTAINTY REGARDING THE SYSTEMA
S INTENSITY FORECAST. UNCERTAINTY REGARDING THE TRACK IS RELATIVELY
LOW OVER THE NEXT 36 HOURS AND INCREASES RAPIDLY BEYOND 48 HOURS DUE
TO UNCERTAINTY REGARDING THE SYSTEM'S INTENSITY.

IN TERMS OF INTENSITY, THE SYSTEM SHOULD BENEFIT FROM FAVORABLE
ENVIRONMENTAL CONDITIONS, WITH SEA SURFACE WATERS AROUND 28C, GOOD
EQUATORIAL UPPER DIVERGENCE, AND LOW VERTICAL WIND SHEAR THROUGH
TOMORROW EVENING. HOWEVER, THE ELONGATED STRUCTURE OF THE CIRCULATION
AND THE LACK OF SURFACE CONVERGENCE MIGHT HINDER THE INTENSIFICATION
IN THE SHORT TERM. LARGE-SCALE DETERMINISTIC AND ENSEMBLE MODELS
THEREFORE REMAIN INCONSISTENT REGARDING A POSSIBLE INTENSIFICATION,
DESPITE A SLIGHT UPWARD TREND IN RECENT RUNS USING THE EPS AND THE
AIFS ENSEMBLE FORECAST. THE HIGH RESOLUTION AROME MODEL AND ITS
ENSEMBLE FORECAST SUGGEST THAT A SMALL SYSTEM COULD INTENSIFY IN THE
SOUTHWESTERN PART OF THE CIRCULATION, ALTHOUGH THERE ARE ALSO
FLUCTUATIONS FROM ONE RUN TO THE NEXT. IT IS THEREFORE POSSIBLE THAT
THE SYSTEM COULD BECOME A TROPICAL STORM BY TOMORROW EVENING,
ALTHOUGH THERE IS STILL SIGNIFICANT UNCERTAINTY. IN THE LONGER TERM,
WESTERLY WIND SHEAR COULD CAUSE THE SYSTEM TO WEAKEN ON SATURDAY AS
AN ALTITUDE TROUGHT PASSES FURTHER SOUTH. THE INTENSITY FORECAST IS
VERY UNCERTAIN BEYOND SATURDAY, EVEN THOUGH THE SYSTEM COULD REMAIN A
TROPICAL STORM IF IT MANAGES TO INTENSIFY BEFORE THEN.

NO SIGNIFICANT IMPACT IS EXPECTED ON INHABITED LAND OVER THE NEXT 72
HOURS.

THE SYSTEM'S CURRENT INTENSITY DOES NOT JUSTIFY THE ISSUANCE OF
REGULAR WARNINGS. NEXT WARNING TOMORROW AT 0600Z.=
