OVER THE PAST 6 TO 12 HOURS, CONVECTION HAS REMAINED VERY SCARCE NEAR
THE SURFACE CENTER, WHICH REMAINS VISIBLE IN THE MULTISPECTRAL
SATELLITE ANIMATION. HOWEVER, CONVECTION APPEARS TO BE PICKING UP
AGAIN BASED ON THE LATEST AVAILABLE IMAGES, WITH FLARING CONVECTION
VISIBLE IN THE SOUTHERN SEMICIRCLE. DUE TO THE LACK OF SIGNIFICANT
IMPROVEMENT IN CONVECTION ORGANIZATION, THE FINAL T IS MAINTAINED AT
1.0, AND MAXIMUM WINDS REMAIN ESTIMATED AT APPROXIMATELY 25 KT.

IN TERMS OF TRACK, THE SYSTEM HAS SHIFTED A BIT MORE WESTWARD TO
NORTHWESTWARD THAN INITIALLY EXPECTED AND SEEMS TO BE SLOWING DOWN A
BIT. IT COULD RESUME ITS SOUTHWESTWARD MOVEMENT OVER THE NEXT 12
HOURS IF CONVECTION MANAGES TO MOVE CLOSER TO LE LOWER-LEVEL CENTER,
WHICH REMAINS QUITE UNCERTAIN. FROM SATURDAY EVENING, THE TRACK
SHOULD HEAD WESTWARD TO WEST-NORTHWESTWARD UNDER THE INFLUENCE OF A
NEW SUBTROPICAL RIDGE SOUTH OF MADAGASCAR. WITH THIS TRAJECTORY, THE
SYSTEM IS EXPECTED TO MAKE LANDFALL IN THE FAR NORTH OF MADAGASCAR BY
SUNDAY EVENING. HOWEVER, THERE IS SIGNIFICANT VARIATION AMONG THE
MODELS REGARDING THE LANDFALL AREA DUE TO A TRAJECTORY THAT DEPENDS
ON THE SYSTEMA  S INTENSITY. THE SYSTEM IS EXPECTED TO MOVE BACK OUT
TO SEA IN THE NORTH OF THE MOZAMBIQUE CHANNEL ON MONDAY, MOST LIKELY
AS AN AREA OF DISTURBED WEATHER.

IN TERMS OF INTENSITY, THE LACK OF SURFACE CONVERGENCE AND THE
PRESENCE OF RELATIVELY COOL (26C) SURFACE WATER NEAR THE SYSTEM ARE
THE MAIN FACTORS LIMITING ANY POSSIBLE INTENSIFICATION. THE FACT THAT
THE SYSTEM IS MOVING FURTHER NORTH THAN INITIALLY FORECAST IS
MARGINALLY FAVORABLE, WITH LOW TO MODERATE VERTICAL SHEAR AND GOOD
DIVERGENCE ON THE NORTHERN EDGE OF THE SUBTROPICAL JET STREAM.
FURTHERMORE, THE SYSTEM COULD BENEFIT FROM MOVING OVER WATERS WITH
HIGHER ENERGY CONTENT NORTH OF TROMELIN TODAY, WHICH COULD ALLOW
CONVECTION TO RESUME NEAR THE CENTER OVER THE NEXT FEW HOURS. IT IS
THEREFORE POSSIBLE THAT THE SYSTEM COULD TEMPORARILY INTENSIFY TO
TROPICAL STORM STRENGTH WITHIN THE NEXT 24 TO 48 HOURS, EVEN THOUGH
THIS SCENARIO IS CURRENTLY RATHER UNLIKELY ACCORDING TO THE VARIOUS
FORECAST MODELS AVAILABLE. THE SYSTEM IS THEREFORE EXPECTED TO
APPROACH THE NORTHERN TIP OF MADAGASCAR AS A TROPICAL DEPRESSION OR
TROPICAL STORM BY SUNDAY EVENING. FROM MONDAY ONWARDS, AFTER
WEAKENING OVER MALAGASY'S TERRAIN, THE SYSTEM SHOULD REMAIN WEAK AS
ITS REMNANTS MOVE OVER THE NORTHERN MOZAMBIQUE CHANNEL.
