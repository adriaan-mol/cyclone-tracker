PART 2 : TROPICAL WEATHER DISCUSSION:

The basin is approaching a Near-Equatorial Trough (NET) pattern east of 55E, although the equatorial westerly flow remains deflected toward the Northern Hemisphere. Convective activity is concentrated mainly around the NET, where it is moderate, it is weaker elsewhere. A baroclinic low-pressure system remains over the southern part of the Mozambique Channel.

The El Nino background, coupled with a weak positive IOD, maintains easterly low-frequency anomalies over the eastern part of the basin. However, an active moist phase of the MJO, coupled with a Kelvin wave, is currently generating a strong westerly burst along the equator and, consequently, favor the NET structure. Initially, the pattern remains biased towards the Northern Hemisphere, where a cyclogenesis is expected to occur.

East of the basin :The NET pattern, oriented toward the Northern Hemisphere, remains unfavorable for cyclogenesis, with limited convergence on the equatorial side. Convective activity will struggle to produce a lasting closed circulation within the NET over the next 5 days. Some ensemble models (EPS, GEFS, AIFS-ENS, etc.) nevertheless suggest a low risk, though it appears to be of little significance at this time.

There is no risk of tropical storm development during the next 5 days.

Over the souther part of the Mozambique Channel :The circulation over the southern part of the Canal still shows no significant convective activity, with an exposed center located near 21.5S / 40.5E. Despite sufficient oceanic potential, weak surface convergence on both the polar and equatorial sides, as well as the presence of a very dry air mass, are limiting this area's potential for development. The models do not indicate a significant risk of tropical transition. This low-pressure system is expected to dissipate by next Monday.

For the next 5 days, there is no potential for this suspect area to develop into a subtropical/tropical storm.



10-day outlook:
Early next week, after the passage of a second Kelvin wave and, more importantly, with the arrival of a vorticity anomaly associated with an equatorial Rossby wave from the east, the NET pattern could become more pronounced, with increased vorticity. Cyclogenesis then cannot be ruled out.
