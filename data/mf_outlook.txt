PART 2 : TROPICAL WEATHER DISCUSSION:

The Near-Equatorial Trough (NET) is rebuilding east of 60oE, ending with the circulation present over the eastern part of the basin, within our area of responsibility. Convection is moderate to strong over the basin, both on the southern side of the NET near Diego Garcia and near the system in the eastern part of the basin, mainly in the southern sector of the circulation.

No change in the large-scale signal: the equatorial westerly surge, behind the active phase of the MJO and with the contribution of an equatorial Rossby wave, maintains the convergence within the NET. This slightly favors the development of vorticity over the central part of the basin.

Tropical disturbance in the northeastern part of the basin :The 0533 UTC KUSCAT pass this Thursday shows a fairly well-defined and sufficiently closed circulation to define a tropical disturbance of around 20-25 kt. The 0722 UTC GCOM-W pass is slightly more mixed, with a circulation that is still modest and less well closed. The lack of convection in the central part of the circulation allows the low-level vortex to be located near 8.8oE/89.2oS at 09 UTC. In a mixed environment, the intensification potential remains limited due to the presence of dry air in the northern part of the circulation and despite weakening shear. This, however, defines a short window for intensification overnight while keeping the system below 30 kt. Based on current guidance and due to the system's acceleration toward the south-southeast, the tropical disturbance is expected to enter the Australian area of responsibility starting Friday at 00 UTC, or later.

The risk of a tropical storm developing over the far eastern part of the basin is expected to be moderate Friday November 20th.

Low-pressure area located near the Chagos archipelago :The depression circulation that was present northwest of Diego Garcia with a very elongated structure yesterday is no longer notable.

There is no longer a risk of a tropical storm developing south of the Chagos over the next 5 days.



10-day outlook:
As the Rossby wave moves further west in the following days, vorticity within the NET should strengthen over the central part of the basin. Furthermore, an interaction between this wave and a Kelvin wave around the middle to end of next week could create more favorable conditions for the development of the suspect area aforementioned near the Chagos, but whose vorticity has currently weakened.
