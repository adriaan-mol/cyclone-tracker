PART 2 : TROPICAL WEATHER DISCUSSION:

The basin displays a Near-Equatorial Trough (NET) configuration east of 60oE. Convective activity is generally weak to moderate near this NET, but locally intense at its eastern tip, near a low-pressure area located near 0.5S/91E within the Indonesian area of responsibility.

The active phase of the MJO and the passage of a Kelvin wave early next week are likely to help sustain the strong westerly wind surge observed along the equator and the current NET pattern. The arrival of an equatorial Rossby wave over the eastern part of the basin is also promoting the formation of two twin vortices on either side of the equator: one in the Bay of Bengal and another very close to the equator near 0.5S/91E. Over the next few days, the gradual westward shift of this Rossby wave (combined temporarily with an MRG wave) is expected to strengthen the northerly component of the near-equatorial flow, thereby promoting greater vorticity over the far northeast of our area of responsibility.

Invest area in the far northeast of the Southwestern Indian Ocean basin:Scatterometric swaths from the past 24 hours and animated satellite imagery from this Sunday suggest the presence of a low-pressure circulation near 0.5S/91E at the border of our area of responsibility, within the Indonesian area of responsibility. The ASCAT-C swath at 0345 UTC shows a closed circulation with maximum winds reaching 20 to 25 kt, despite its proximity to the equator and the presence of a competing cyclogenesis in the Bay of Bengal. Conditions for the development of this circulation are currently uncertain, with good oceanic potential and a moist environment but limited surface convergence efficiency due to proximity to the equator and moderate to strong northeasterly wind shear at the western boundary of the invest area. Over the next few days, the invest area is expected to track slowly south-southwestward and return within our area of responsibility. Surface convergence is expected to improve; however, northeasterly wind shear is expected to remain moderate to strong over this period. Conditions for intensification thus remain uncertain, with a relatively weak signal of development with most models, except for the European Ensemble Model (EPS), which shows a moderate to strong signal over the next five days.

The risk of tropical storm development over the far northeastern part of the basin becomes low from Tuesday November17th.



10-day outlook:
As the Rossby wave moves further west in the following days, convergence within the NET should strengthen over the central part of the basin. Another suspect area could form further west from the week starting on November 23rd somewhere to the east of the Seychelles or Agalega.
