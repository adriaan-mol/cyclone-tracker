PART 2 : TROPICAL WEATHER DISCUSSION:

The Near-Equatorial Trough (NET) present over the eastern basin has split into two due to the action of two low-pressure circulations. Two branches are distinguishable: one extending from the Seychelles to the Chagos, bounded to the east by a poorly defined elongated circulation; the other east of 85oE, reaching the vicinity of the Zone of Disturbed weather located near the border with the Indonesian Area of Responsibility.Moderate to strong convection over the basin is concentrated south of the sheared area of disturbed weather in the eastern part of the basin (see below), while convection around the circulation northwest of Diego Garcia has significantly weakened. Locally moderate activity is also present north of the Mozambique Channel.

The current equatorial westerly wind surge should keep on going in the coming days, due to the combination of the westerly anomaly in the wake of the active MJO, and to the contribution of a westward-moving equatorial Rossby wave between the east and the centre of the basin. This set-up brings increasing convergence within the NET and a better equatorial feed over the north-east of the basin.

Suspect area in the northeastern part of the basin :A low-level vortex is now clearly distinguishable in visible satellite imagery around 7oS/89oE around 10 UTC.This vortex is located about 90 NM from its convection, which has been displaced much further south by strong mid-level shear. The ASCAT-B swath from this Wednesday at 0223 UTC shows a fairly well-defined circulation already far from the convective envelope, with average wind speeds between 25 and 30 knots in the gradient zone south of the center. The system can no longer be classified using the Dvorak technique, as the center is too far from the dense cloud formation.The strong mid-level northerly shear currently in place is expected to persist until Friday before weakening slightly, also in relation with the southward movement of the low-level vortex. Meanwhile, the deep-layer northeasterly shear remains well-established until Friday. This weekend, upper-level divergence is also expected to improve, but according to most modelsexcept for the IFS 00 UTC and its EPS 00 UTC ensemblethe area of disturbed weather is likely to be located within the Australian Area of  Responsibility.There remain a few intensification scenarios (mainly in the GEFS and EPS physical ensemble models, and very few from AI models) to a moderate tropical storm stage this Friday before the system exits our area of responsibility (prior to a potential return as a remnant low on next Sunday or Monday).

The risk of a tropical storm developing over the far eastern part of the basin is expected to be low Friday November 20th.

Low-pressure area located near the Chagos archipelago :The low-pressure circulation present northwest of Diego Garcia within the Near-Equatorial Trough (NET) is now highly elongated and divided into two poorly defined vorticity poles. At this stage, no scatterometer imagery can be effectively used, and convection has decreased over the past 24 hours.Improvement will depend on the arrival of the equatorial-Rossby wave in the middle of the basin early next week, which could enhance convergence in this area. The upper-level environment may then feature strong upper-level divergence along a corridor between the Chagos and Seychelles, with vertical wind shear expected to remain low. Sea surface temperatures in the area are abnormally warm (around 29-30oC). However, uncertainties remain regarding the effectiveness of low-level convergence and the consolidation of the current low-level precursor, which could potentially compete with other precursors within the NET.Even the most aggressive deterministic models (ICON and ARPEGE) suggest a tropical storm forming early next week, while most other deterministic models are less responsive. Both AI and physical ensemble models are in good agreement, with a minority of members (10 to 40 %) reaching the moderate tropical storm stage south of Diego Garcia by mid-next week.

The risk of tropical storm development to the south of Chagos archipelago is expected to become very low from Monday November 23rd.



10-day outlook:
As the Rossby wave moves further west in the following days, vorticity within the NET should strengthen over the central part of the basin. Furthermore, an interaction between this wave and a Kelvin wave around the middle to end of next week could create more favorable conditions for the development of the suspect area aforementioned near the Chagos.However, the cyclogenesis timespan is quite limited due to the disappearance of the moist MJO signal over the Maritime Continent.
