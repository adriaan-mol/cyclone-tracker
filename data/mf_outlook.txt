PART 2 : TROPICAL WEATHER DISCUSSION:

The basin exhibits a Near-Equatorial Trough (NET) pattern east of 60E. Convective activity is generally weak in the western part of the NET, but it is much more pronounced near the eastern edge, in the vicinity of a low-pressure system located within the Indonesian area of responsibility near 1S/92E.

The combination of an active MJO phase, the passage of a Kelvin wave, and the arrival of the Equatorial Rossby wave from the east has led to the development of a strong westerly flow and two twin circulations on either side of the equator.Over the next few days, the active MJO context should maintain the NET over the central basin, while the gradual westward shift of the Rossby wave should promote a good equatorial feed over the far northeast of our area of responsibility.

Suspect area in the Indonesian AoR : The 0302UTC ASCAT-B pass indicates the presence of a large closed circulation with maximum winds approaching 30 kt, despite its proximity to the equator. The significant convective activity observed this morning has since weakened considerably. The circulation is currently moving southward. It could turn southwestward and enter our area of responsibility sometime tomorrowConditions are currently still unfavorable for its development due to strong easterly wind shear and its proximity to the equator. Equatorial and polar convergence, however, remains fairly good. As the system shifts southward, this environment should improve, notably with an increase in upper-level divergence and a decrease in wind shear. That said, northerly mid-level wind shear could remain sufficiently strong (particularly on Wednesday) to limit the system's development. On Thursday, the decrease of the wind shear could once again allow the system to intensify before a likely definitive increase in westerly to northwesterly deep-level shear.Numerical models are in relative agreement regarding the possible transition of this system into our area of responsibility by tomorrow evening. However, they disagree on the potential for intensification, particularly in the short term. In the longer term, most models agree on a low to moderate risk, as shown by certain ensembles.

The risk of a tropical storm entering/developing over the far northeastern part of the basin becomes low from Tuesday November 17th and moderate from Thursday November 19th.



10-day outlook:
As the Rossby wave moves further west in the following days, vorticity within the NET should strengthen over the central part of the basin. Another suspect area could thus form further west starting Sunday November 22 somewhere to the east of Agalega.
