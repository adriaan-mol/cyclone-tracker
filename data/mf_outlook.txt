PART 2 : TROPICAL WEATHER DISCUSSION:

Apart from Tropical Depression No. 02-20262027 northeast of Madagascar, the basin no longer contains any other systems. However, a poorly defined near-equatorial trough (NET) extends between 60oE and 80oE along 04oS. Convective activity is moderate within 02-20262027, as monitored by the CMRS, and moderate in the far northeast of the basin, within a zone of trade wind convergence.

The El Nino context, coupled with a weak positive IOD, maintains easterly low-frequency anomalies over the eastern part of the basin. The westerly wind anomaly initiated by the humid MJO, which will cross the basin over the next 5 days, is not sufficient to reverse this easterly anomaly. The interaction of Rossby-Kelvin-MRG-MJO waves in the western part of the basin creates favorable conditions for Tropical Depression 02-2026227, currently being tracked, by enhancing vorticity and convection. However, this will not be sufficient to withstand the strong deep-layer shear. Further east, the westerly push from the MJO and the passage of a Kelvin wave help improve the structure of the NET by mid-next week.

Some members of the ensemble models suggest a cyclogenesis near Diego Garcia in an area where vertical wind shear tends to decrease and where the NET could strengthen. However, the low-level convergence is still too weak to allow for a closed circulation. Additionally, AI models do not support this cyclogenesis scenario. Thus, the risk of cyclogenesis is assessed as very low near Diego Garcia.

The risk of a moderate tropical storm forming becomes very low starting from Wenesday, November 11, near Diego Garcia.



10-day outlook:
The NET configuration is expected to re-establish itself, driven by a Kelvin wave traveling across the basin around mid-November.
